# Problem Statement

**Hackathon:** Multimodal AI Hackathon 2026 (KamandPrompt, IIT Mandi), hosted on Devpost
**Track B:** Mapping Flood Damage from Space

This file restates the organisers' Track B problem statement. Treat it as the source of truth for what must be built. Rules and scoring are in `RULES_AND_CRITERIA.md`.

## Challenge

When roads, bridges and phone lines are gone, rescuers still need to know where the damage is and who is cut off. Build the system that tells them, from space.

## Context

- On 26 August 2026, the partial collapse of a high-altitude glacier in northern Nepal triggered an avalanche of ice and rock that sent a flood of water, mud and debris down the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli river corridor.
- Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority reported 1,342 deaths by 6 September, with almost 4,900 people still missing or out of contact.
- In disasters like this, ground sensors are swept away, and roads, bridges and communications fail. Rescuers urgently need to know where the damage is worst and which villages have been cut off.
- Free satellite data covers every Himalayan valley. Radar satellites see through monsoon clouds, and optical satellites show damage in detail. The challenge is turning that raw data into trustworthy answers quickly enough to matter.

## Requirements

Build a system that takes **an area and a flood date** and uses satellite images to answer three questions for rescue teams.

### 1. Where did the flood hit?

Map flooded and debris-covered areas by comparing images from before and after the event. Use Sentinel-1 radar, which sees through monsoon clouds, and Sentinel-2 optical images when the sky is clear.

### 2. What was damaged?

Overlay buildings, roads and bridges from OpenStreetMap and estimate which ones were hit.

### 3. Who is cut off?

Using the road network, find settlements that no longer have a road connection to the nearest town or hospital.

### AI component (choose one)

- **Flood segmentation model:** train a model to detect water or debris in satellite images using the listed training data, and show how well it works on Himalayan scenes it has never seen.
- **Situation-report copilot:** an assistant that answers rescuers' questions and writes short situation reports in English and Nepali. Every number must come from the system's maps, never invented by the language model.

### Bonus

Given any point upstream, trace the flood path down the valley using elevation data and list the settlements along it.

### Case study

Map the August 2026 Trishuli flood and compare the results with the official Copernicus Emergency Management Service maps for this event (activation EMSR927).

## Expected deliverables

- A map dashboard, plus a one-page situation report generated by the system
- GitHub repository with a README explaining how to run the system
- Report (max. 6 pages), including a limitations section that explains what the system cannot do. Example given by the organisers: Sentinel satellites revisit an area only every few days, so they cannot warn of a sudden glacier collapse minutes ahead.
- 3-minute demo video
- Required data attributions and training-dataset citations
- Case-study results compared with the EMSR927 reference maps

## Technical considerations

- The system must run end to end from raw satellite data for **any area and date the judges choose**.
- Organisers' tip: Sentinel-1 radar images of the corridor exist from a few days before and after the event. For change detection, compare images from the **same orbit track** (same time of day, 12 days apart). Images from different tracks view the terrain from different angles and cannot be compared pixel by pixel.

## Datasets named by the organisers

|---|---|---|
| Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 | Before/after images | dataspace.copernicus.eu |
| OpenStreetMap, pre-event snapshot | Buildings, roads, bridges | ohsome API, https://api.ohsome.org/v1 (snapshot before 26 Aug 2026; latest available at time of writing is 27 July 2026) |

## A note on responsibility

This disaster is recent and many families are still affected. Treat the topic with respect: do not use images of victims in demos or reports. Systems built here are educational prototypes, not operational tools.
